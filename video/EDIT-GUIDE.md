# Edit guide for Final Cut Pro

Everything below is prepared so that the edit is assembly, not invention. You are putting real clips onto a timeline that
already has the right length, the right beats, the captions and the effects ready.

**Realistic time for one editor:** about 2½ hours for the full cut, about 1 hour for the minimum cut (see the end).
Footage must be in by **about 14:00 Finnish time** for the 15:00 deadline.

## What is ready

| Material | Where | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| **Assembly project** | `video/final-cut/RoadDesk-assembly.fcpxml` | A 1920×1080, 30 fps project of 2:50 cut into 16 named placeholders (one per beat), a chapter marker at each scene and a **to-do marker on every beat** saying what to film, the words, the sound, and which screen recording to use. |
| **Captions** | `video/subtitles.srt` | 30 English cues timed to the script. Retime after the cut (below). |
| **Caption cards** | `video/assets/cards/*.png` | Transparent 1920×1080 overlays in the app’s own look: “09:00. A calm shift.”, the three “THE SIMULATOR” cards, “1 needs you”, the estimates footnote, the “simulated” tags, the title card, and a hazard triangle sprite (512×512). |
| **Sound effects** | `video/assets/sfx/*.wav` | 11 synthesized effects: pop, click, alarm sting, panic pulse, relief chord, snare riser, hazard tick, timelapse build, car ride, engine off, mug down. Made by us, no licences. |
| **Music** | `video/MUSIC.md` | Cue sheet and shortlist. **Not downloaded**: you pick by ear. |
| **Storyboard** | the “Road Desk video storyboard” deck | One sketch per beat, for the shoot. |
| **Slides** | the “Road Desk presentation” deck | Export the business, limits and next-steps slides as stills; the last slide is the end card. |

The assembly project, the captions and the effects are all regenerated by scripts in `video/tools/` if you change the script
(`make-fcpxml.py`, `make-cards.cjs`, `make-sfx.sh`).

## Before you open Final Cut

1. Copy the whole `video/` folder from the repository to the laptop that will edit.
2. Put raw footage in `footage/<beat id>_<what>_<take>.mov` (the shoot is grouped by location, L1 to L4: see `video/SHOOT-PLAN.md`), for example `2c_driver-face_take2.mov`. The beat id first keeps the
   browser in story order. Screen recordings go in `screens/` as `R1_keys.mov`, `R2_run-A.mov` and so on (the list is in
   `video/SCRIPT.md`, “Screen recordings to capture first”).
3. Export the three slides you need as PNG from the presentation deck, and the end card, into `video/assets/cards/` or beside them.

## Step by step

**1. Open the project.** *File ▸ Import ▸ XML…* and choose `RoadDesk-assembly.fcpxml`. You get an event “Road Desk video”
with a project “Road Desk assembly”. Open the project; the timeline shows the 16 named placeholders. Open *Timeline Index ▸
Tags* to see the to-do markers; tick each one off as the beat is covered. (I could not open Final Cut here, so this file is
untested in the app. If the import is refused, skip the file and use the table in this guide: the same beats and times are in
`video/SCRIPT.md`.)

**1b. The screen clips come from scripts.** `video/tools/capture/` records the screen parts and `frame.cjs` puts them in a phone or
window frame at 1080p. Each run also writes an `events.json`: the agent’s real messages with times, and where every clip starts, so
the phone, the operator’s screen and the demo panel line up. Use the framed clips; keep the raw ones for punch-ins.

**2. Import everything.** *File ▸ Import ▸ Media…* for footage, screens, `cards/` and `sfx/`. Turn on *Keywords from Finder
tags* only if you used them. Do *not* tick “copy to library” for the full-size footage if the disk is tight; do tick it for
the cards and sound effects (they are small).

**3. Import the captions.** Select the project, then *File ▸ Import ▸ Captions…* and choose `subtitles.srt`. They land as a
caption lane. You will retime them in step 8.

**4. Pass one, the dialogue and the picture (about 40 minutes).** Work left to right through the beats. For each one,
pick the best take (press **F** to favourite, **X** to reject), mark the part you want with **I** and **O**, and lay it onto
the primary storyline with **D** (overwrite) or **E** (append). Do not worry about length yet: if a beat runs a second or two
over, that is fine. Delete a placeholder when its beat is covered (select it, **Shift+Delete** ripple-deletes).

**5. Pass two, the screen inserts (about 30 minutes).** For every beat that has a screen recording, edit the insert as a
**connected clip** above the main shot: park the playhead, mark the insert, press **Q** (connect). Keep them 2–4 seconds. The
real screens are the proof, so cut to them as soon as the line is said. Put each insert **over** the actor’s line so the sound
carries on.

**6. Pass three, sound (about 30 minutes).** The voice-over was recorded in the pod, so put it down first, on its own lane, and cut the picture to it. Assign roles first: select all the dialogue clips and use *Modify ▸ Assign Audio
Roles ▸ Dialogue*, the effects *Effects*, the music *Music*. Then:
- Clean the dialogue: in the Audio inspector, turn on **Reduce Noise** and **Equalization ▸ Voice Enhance**; set peaks
  around −10 dB on the meter. If you recorded a clap, use *Clip ▸ Synchronize Clips* to line up separate audio.
- Lay the room tone under every cut so the silence does not jump.
- Place the effects from `sfx/` where the beat notes say (the to-do markers list them). Shorter is funnier: trim the pop and
  the click tight to the frame the button is pressed.
- Put `car-ride.wav` under 2c, then `engine-off.wav` at the moment the driver switches off, then `hazard-tick.wav` under 2d.

**7. Pass four, the cards (about 20 minutes).** Drag a card PNG above the shot it belongs to and stretch it to the length of
the beat. The beat markers name the right card. To make the three “THE SIMULATOR” cards pop in, add two keyframes to *Scale*
(90 % to 100 % over 6 frames) and a fade-in on the opacity. The hazard triangle: place it small in the dashboard corner and
keyframe *Opacity* 0 % ↔ 100 % about every 15 frames, or just tap the spare phone during filming and skip this.

**8. Retime the captions (about 15 minutes).** The captions are timed to the script, not to your final cut. Open the caption
lane, select all the captions of one scene, and drag them as a block to match the audio, then fix the individual ones in the
Caption inspector. Keep each caption on screen for at least 1.5 seconds and no more than two lines. Read the whole film with
the sound off once.

**9. Pass five, music (about 20 minutes).** Place each cue where `MUSIC.md` says; trim to length; fade in and out
(drag the fade handles). Set the music about 18–22 dB under the dialogue and lower it further under every line with volume
keyframes (Option-click on the volume line). Use *Audio ▸ Show Audio Lanes* to keep it tidy.

**10. Look at it.** Total length must be **under 3:00 including the end card.** Colour: if the clips differ, select one and
use *Match Color* on the others; lower the exposure a little on the dark-room scene (beat 3a) so the map glows.

**11. Export.** *File ▸ Share ▸ Master File*, set the format to *Computer*, video codec *H.264 Faster Encode*, resolution
1920×1080. On the *Roles* tab, set the captions to **Burn In** so the English subtitles are in the picture (judges may watch
with the sound off). Name it `road-desk-demo.mp4`. Check the file once on a phone before you upload it. Paste the music credits from `video/CREDITS.md` into the video description.

## Keys you will use all day

| Key | What it does |
|---|---|
| **I / O** | Set the start / end of the part you want |
| **E / D / Q / W** | Append / overwrite / connect above / insert |
| **F / X / U** | Favourite / reject / clear |
| **B** and **Cmd+B** | Blade tool / cut at the playhead |
| **Shift+Delete** | Ripple delete (closes the gap) |
| **J K L** | Play backward / stop / forward (tap again for speed) |
| **Cmd+Z** | Undo; edit fearlessly |
| **Cmd+T** | Add the default transition to a cut (use sparingly) |
| **Option-click on the volume line** | Add a volume keyframe |
| **N** | Snapping on and off (turn it off for fine moves) |

## If you are running late: the minimum cut (about 1 hour)

Drop these in this order, and tell nobody:
1. **2g, the chaos montage.** Keep only the last shot (“Not now.”).
2. **The 5b limits and next steps** to 15 seconds; keep “prototype, simulated, no login yet, a shadow pilot next”.
3. **The roleplay’s B-roll:** keep only the lines, the real screen inserts and the cards.
4. **The all-three shots:** if the lobby shots look poor, use one still frame of the thumbs-up for two seconds at each end.
5. **The timelapse:** if it is not ready, use 15 seconds of the live map with the clock jumped forward (“+60 min” a few times)
   and the `tag-simulated.png` card.
6. **Music:** one track under the whole film is better than five clumsy ones.

Never drop: the numbers and the footnote, the real screen inserts, the process segment, the limits sentence, the end card.

## Asking for edit help

I can help with things that are text or timing, not with the footage itself:
- **Retime the captions.** Paste the list of your final cut’s start times per beat and I will produce a new `.srt`.
- **Fit the words to the time.** If a segment runs long, paste how long it is and I will trim the voice-over to fit.
- **Rebuild the assembly project with real clips.** Give me the file names and their lengths (from *Show in Finder* or
  `ffprobe`) and I will write an `.fcpxml` that already has them on the timeline in order.
- **Check the cut against the brief.** Send me the beat list with times and I will check the total, the footnote, and that
  the process and limits are covered.
