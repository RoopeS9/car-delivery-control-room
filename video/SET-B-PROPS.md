# Set B: the car, with no car

The driver scene (beats 2c, 2d and 2e) is built from chairs and kitchen things. This works because the film is already a
comedy, with a simulator gremlin and an operator with their feet up: a plate for a steering wheel is on tone. Do not hide
the low budget; frame it tight and play it straight. Panic reads the same in a cardboard car as in a real one.

## What to gather (about 15 minutes)

| Prop | Stands in for | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Two chairs side by side** (or a sofa end) | The front seats | The driver sits on the **left**: the story is set in Finland and Germany, and both drive on the right. |
| **A paper plate with holes cut in it** | The steering wheel | Cut three or four holes so it reads as spokes. Fix it to a small box or a stack of books so it sits at chest height, tilted a little toward the driver. A round baking tray or a bike wheel also works. |
| **A long box, an ironing board or a table edge, with a dark cloth over it** | The dashboard | Put it between the wheel and the camera. |
| **A tablet, laptop or second phone** | The instrument cluster | Full-screen `starter/public/sim/dashboard-misfire.jpg` (the app’s own drawn misfire dashboard). Tuck it behind the wheel with the screen facing the driver and a little toward the camera. |
| **A small orange light** | The glow of the warning light | A phone torch under orange paper or a sweet wrapper, or the tablet’s own glow. Dim the room a little so it shows. |
| **A spare phone** showing `video/assets/cards/hazard-triangle.png` | The hazard lights | Full-screen, brightness up. Flash it at about once a second by tapping, or add the flashing in the edit. |
| **A scarf or a belt** | The seat belt | Optional, but it is a good small detail. |
| **A blanket or a sheet** hung behind the actors | The windows | A plain, bright backdrop. Do not show a real room. |
| **The driver’s own phone** | Leo’s phone | The real app on screen for the insert shots. |

## The angles (shoot all of these, they are quick)

1. **Over the wheel, from the passenger side, a little low.** The driver’s face, the plate and the lit dashboard in one frame.
   This is the money shot for 2c. A teammate can hold the phone in their lap.
2. **Close on the driver’s face** for the panic, then again for the relief. Fill the frame so the room disappears.
3. **Close on the dashboard glow**: the tablet showing the warning light, with the plate in front.
4. **Close on the driver’s hands** gripping the plate, then lifting off.
5. **Close on the driver’s phone**, a hand typing. Use the real app (or screen-record it and insert it in the edit).
6. **A wide shot**, once, only as a reveal at the very end of the scene if you want the joke: the whole cardboard car.

## Make it feel like a car

- **Shake:** someone sitting behind gently rocks the chair back and forth during the panic. The camera operator adds a
  small hand shake. Stop everything the moment the driver “pulls over”.
- **Sound:** put `video/assets/sfx/car-ride.wav` under 2c, cut it with `engine-off.wav` when the driver switches off, and
  let `video/assets/sfx/hazard-tick.wav` run through 2d. The alarm sting and pulse are in the same folder. (The ride and
  engine-off sounds are synthesized placeholders: judge them by ear and replace them if they are not convincing.)
- **Light:** warm, a little dim, with the dashboard as the brightest thing. Avoid windows behind the actor.
- **Speed:** in the edit, a quick cut every 1–2 seconds in 2c makes it feel urgent even though nothing moves.

## If a real car turns up

Film it parked, hazards on, daylight, away from strangers and number plates, and never while moving. Everything
else in the script and the edit stays the same.

## Safety

Chairs on a flat floor, nobody standing on one, nobody rocking hard. No driving of any kind for the video.
