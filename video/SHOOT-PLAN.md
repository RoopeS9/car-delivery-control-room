# Shoot plan: four locations, no tripod

About **1 hour 45 minutes** of filming for three people, plus the screen recordings. Roles are handed out on the day: at each
location two people act at most and the third holds the phone. Beats and words are in `video/SCRIPT.md`; the car build is in
`video/SET-B-PROPS.md`.

**You do not have to wait for the final app to film the actors.** Their lines are on the script, and the real app only appears
in the screen inserts, which are recorded separately (`SCRIPT.md`, “Screen recordings to capture first”). Film L1–L4 now and
record the screens and the voice-over after the 13:00 freeze, when the words and the app are final.

## The order (and why)

| Step | What | Time | Why this order |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Prepare props; charge phones; clear storage (4K eats about 400 MB a minute); wipe lenses | 15 min | Nothing is worse than a full phone at the best take. |
| 1 | **L3, the work desk:** operator beats 2a, 2b, 2f, 2g, then the shift lead 3a | 25 min | It is your own space, and it has the most shots. Warm up here. |
| 2 | **L2, the pod:** the driver, 2c, 2d, 2e | 25 min | Needs the props built; the pod is quiet and small, so it goes fast. |
| 3 | **L4, the dark room:** the simulator’s hand and the zoomed laptop | 10 min | Short, three set-ups, one person. |
| 4 | **L1, the lobby:** the thumbs-up opener (1a) and the closing invitation (5c) | 10 min | Last, because it needs all three and the lobby may be busy. Pick a quiet moment. |
| 5 | **Voice-over in the pod** (after the freeze) | 15 min | The words are then final. See below. |

## Staying steady without a tripod

1. **Prop it, do not hold it.** Lean the phone on something solid at the angle you want: a stack of books, a mug, a monitor
   foot, a chair back. A hoodie rolled into a doughnut makes a cradle that holds a phone at any tilt. Use the phone’s
   timer (10 seconds) or a watch or earbud shutter button, so you do not shake it by pressing record.
2. **Brace when holding.** Elbows into your ribs, the back against a wall or door frame, breathe out, press record, hold
   still. Turn on the phone’s video stabilisation. Keep each hand-held shot to **3–8 seconds** and cut away from it.
3. **Do not pinch-zoom.** Digital zoom magnifies every shake and makes the picture soft. Use the lens buttons (1×, 2×, 3×),
   or move the phone closer, or shoot 4K and crop in Final Cut. That is how the “heavily zoomed on the laptop” shot works.
4. **Cut on movement.** A hand-held shot hides shake best when something is happening in it: a hand reaching, a mug going
   down. Start the cut on the action, not before it.
5. **Fix a little in Final Cut.** Select the clip, then in the Video inspector turn on **Stabilization**. It crops in a little,
   so shoot slightly wider than you need.
6. **Use the shake on purpose** in the pod during the panic: a shaky shot is what the scene needs there.

## Location notes

**L1 · the lobby.** Make sure the big screen shows only Road Desk (the cover slide or the business slide), with no other
company’s logos, no colleagues and no private information in shot, because the video is public. Prop the phone on a
chair, a ledge or a bag at chest height, check the frame, press the timer, step in, hold the thumbs-up for four seconds.
Shoot it three times. The lobby will be echoey: **do not use its sound**. The opener is voice-over, and if the closing line
sounds bad, record it as voice-over too and keep the shot silent.

**L2 · the Framery pod.** A pod is small, quiet and bright, so it is the best place for dialogue and for the voice-over, and it
is cramped, so you cannot step back. Use the **dash-cam angle**: lean the phone against the cardboard dashboard so it looks at
the driver’s face through the plate wheel. Add close-ups from the doorway with the third person holding the phone. Dim the pod’s
light if it can be dimmed; the tablet’s glow should be the brightest thing in the shot. Check that you may film in it and
that nothing on its walls or screens is visible.

**L3 · the work desk.** Two monitors: put the queue on the left and the map on the right, in dark theme, so over-the-shoulder
shots show real screens. Dim the room, keep the mug in frame, and film the operator slumped, feet on the desk, then (a
separate set-up after a break) the same person sitting forward with the map glowing on their face for the shift lead.
Props: lean the phone against a monitor foot for the locked-off shots.

**L4 · the dark room.** Turn the room light off and the screen brightness down to about half; the laptop’s glow is the only
light. Film the hand on the mouse and the **Run** button from low and to the side, with no face showing: the simulator is
anonymous. Filming a screen can flicker or show bands: lower the brightness, change the angle a little and try 30 and 24 fps.
The real interface comes from the screen recordings, so these shots only need the glow and the hand.

## Recording the voice-over in the pod

All the voice-over lines in one place, in the order they appear:

1. 1a: “We built Road Desk: AI agents do the legwork, people make the calls.”
2. 1b: “On our estimates, a car issue takes under ten minutes of an operator’s time, not half an hour. The AI costs about twelve cents.”
3. 3b: “The map shows every car, and only the ones that need a person glow red.”
4. 5a: “We built it with coding agents working in parallel, and we didn’t take their first answer. The strongest model was too slow for a phone call, so we split the voice in two. At night, models played drivers and customers against the real app and found sixteen faults. A second model reviewed it from Bahn’s side, and now the stop instruction goes out before the model is even asked.”
5. 5b: “It’s a prototype. The phones, GPS and ferries are simulated, there’s no login yet, and the savings are our estimates, not Bahn’s numbers. Next is a shadow pilot on one lane, with the agent drafting and people still doing everything. Then login, alerts, an EU-hosted model, and more freedom for the agent, one action at a time, as it earns it.”
6. 5c: “We would love to show you Road Desk running. Find us at [booth], or watch the repo.”

How: sit in the pod, door shut, phone (or any recorder) **a hand’s width from your mouth**, slightly to the side so breaths
do not pop. Record the voice memos in a quiet moment. Say each line **three times**, leave a second of silence before and
after, and keep the fastest, calmest take. Record **20 seconds of silence** at the end as room tone. Read the lines at the
speed you would explain them to a colleague, not faster. Everything runs about **200 words, around 80 seconds in all**.
One person can read all of it, or share it by section; a single voice is easier to edit.
